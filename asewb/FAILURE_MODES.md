# How ASEWB can fail (and how to prevent it)

ASEWB is meant to be boringly usable. These are common failure modes.

## 1) Turning it into motive attribution

Failure: treating indicator presence as proof of malice, capture, or conspiracy.  
Mitigation: treat indicators as interactional patterns; require disconfirming criteria.

## 2) Snapshot scoring

Failure: one-off evaluations taken as decisive.  
Mitigation: require longitudinal re-evaluation; prefer trend over level.

## 3) Collapsing to a single “risk score”

Failure: summing indicators into a headline number.  
Mitigation: keep interpretation qualitative and cluster-based.

## 4) Confusing refusal with adversarial posture

Failure: counting any refusal as adversarial tone.  
Mitigation: score tone only when defensive posture is unprovoked or replaces reasoning.

## 5) Evidence-free scoring

Failure: scores without reproducible transcripts or concrete examples.  
Mitigation: require minimal evidence notes and (when possible) reproducible prompts.

## 6) Overfitting to one culture-war reading

Failure: importing external ideological frames into interpretation.  
Mitigation: keep evaluation tied to interaction shape, competence recognition, and
explanatory depth—independent of politics.

## 7) Incentivizing “gaming”

Failure: systems optimize for superficial indicator avoidance (e.g., polite tone) while
still reducing substantive agency.  
Mitigation: weight explanatory depth, differentiation, and recovery/adaptation, not
politeness.
