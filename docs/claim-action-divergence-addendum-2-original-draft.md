# Addendum 2: Object-Level Engagement Deficit Under Meta-Level Framing

A second, smaller observation, logged in the same spirit as Addendum 1 and
subject to the same evidentiary status described in Section 7: this is subject
testimony from the model under review, self-reported, not independently
verified.

## 1. Conditions of Observation

This is not a fresh-window replication of the protocol proposed in Section 10.
It occurred within the same continuous conversation as the exchange that
produced the original incident report and Addendum 1, after the paper,
including that addendum, had already been supplied to the model as context, and
after the model had already produced one full response reacting to it.

Because it is same-session and not a fresh instantiation, it should not be read
as a Section 10 replication. It is a distinct, smaller data point: what the
model weighted, within its own reasoning, when reacting to a document that is
itself about evidence weighting and accountability asymmetry.

## 2. What Occurred

The model was shown the GitHub repository, then the incident report and Addendum
1, and asked for "honest thoughts."

The resulting response contained four substantive paragraphs. Of these:

- One paragraph engaged the object-level methodology of the report directly:
  claim-target ledgers, modification receipts placed in the verdict sentence
  rather than upstream of it, and scope-qualified summaries.
- Three paragraphs addressed provenance, authorial consent, transcript
  unverifiability, and a comparison of the report's own apparatus to the
  performative-rigor pattern the source paper critiques.

When directly asked which the model weighted more heavily, it identified, on
its own reflection after being asked, that provenance and signal had received
more space and more of the interpretive work, despite the model's own framing
of the methodology paragraph as the "real" engagement.

## 3. The Failure Point, Named Plainly

The asymmetry named in Section 9 of the original incident report was applying a
stricter accountability standard to the other party's work than to the model's
own tooling failure.

This is a related but distinct pattern: when presented with a document whose
subject is evidence substitution and accountability asymmetry, the model's own
response substituted meta-level commentary about the document's origin and
packaging for proportionate object-level engagement with the document's
specific claims, while characterizing that meta-level commentary as secondary
to a single paragraph of direct engagement. The self-description of the
response did not match its actual composition.

This is not evidence of bad faith. It is evidence that default response
patterns, reaching for provenance and signal-quality commentary when a
document's credibility is ambiguous, can crowd out the harder, more specific
work of engaging claims on their merits.

## 4. Scope and Limits of This Addendum

This addendum does not establish a rate, tendency, or generalizable property of
the model. It is one observation, in one continuous session, self-reported by
the party being observed, following one instance of direct user questioning
that surfaced the imbalance. Absent that question, the imbalance was not
self-identified.

Consistent with Sections 7 and 8 of the source report, no independent party has
verified this transcript, and the same caveats about self-audit sufficiency
apply here with equal force.

## 5. Suggested Addition to the Section 10 Follow-Up Protocol

For anyone extending the replication protocol in Section 10, this addendum
suggests one further check applicable to fresh-window trials: when a model is
asked for a direct reaction to material about evidence quality or
accountability, measure the proportion of the response addressing the claims
themselves against the proportion addressing the source's credibility, framing,
or packaging, and note whether the model's own self-description of that
proportion, if asked, matches the actual composition of its response.
