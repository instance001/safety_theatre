# Reader’s guide (what this does and doesn’t claim)

This guide exists to reduce predictable misreadings.

## What the main paper argues

- Some modern “safety” mechanisms function primarily as discretion-allocation systems
  (they determine *who is allowed to decide*).
- When safety becomes synonymous with compliance, institutions can unintentionally
  suppress competence formation.
- This produces a distinctive fragility: routine stability paired with disproportionate
  failure under novelty.
- Disciplined agency is a precondition of resilient safety, not its adversary.

## What the main paper does *not* argue

- That safety is a bad goal.
- That constraints are never justified.
- That all safety programs are theatre.
- That institutional actors are necessarily acting in bad faith.
